# Self-Healing-Hydrogel
Fabrication and characterization of a self-healing PVA–Chitosan hydrogel crosslinked with borax
This project covers the design, fabrication, and testing of a self-healing hydrogel built from polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) and chitosan, crosslinked with borax. The aim was to produce a soft, stretchable material that behaves like soft tissue and repairs itself after being cut, with no external trigger such as heat or pressure. The gel was made by blending a 4% PVA solution with 1% chitosan and then adding borax dropwise to form reversible borate ester crosslinks. These dynamic bonds, together with hydrogen bonding from chitosan, are what allow cut surfaces to knit back together at room temperature. Samples were cast into silicone molds and porcelain crucibles, cured overnight, and tested under compression on a Comprehensive Material Testing Apparatus (ME 8236), as fresh specimens and again after a healing period. Performance was judged against two CEP targets: an elastic recovery above 80% and a healing efficiency of at least 45%. The fabricated gel was translucent, flexible, and showed visible self-healing within about 24 hours of the cut surfaces being rejoined. Measured elastic recovery was about 88% and healing efficiency reached about 70% in compressive strength, which met the required specifications. The results show that a simple borax-crosslinked PVA-chitosan system can deliver autonomous self-healing suitable for soft, tissuemimetic applications.
